Overworld multiplayer

#1 - Raidmode / Worldbased Questsystem
For overworld multiplayer, the states of the worlds, including certain story points, quests, etc. need to be saved on the Relay/Server.
Players joining would work together to complete the story. 

Downsides: 
Events would only be cleared by 1 player this way. this includes companions joining the party via story.
Story based recruitment would not work - auto recruit on reaching a certain level instead.


#2 - MMO / Instance based Progression
Maps, events or even certain story points would be flagged as public or private.
A few maps would be flagged as public in which the player is able to see other players.

The story and events are all played through by every player and saved on their own save.
Players can play together when they form a party on a shared map. The party leader's save would be used for story progression.
This means that the party members temporarily receive the Party leader's progression data but would ultimately act as guests in their world.

Checks could be made so that if both players share the same story state at party creation, the party member would also permanently advance their own story.

Downsides: 
Need to make sure both players are always on the same map when events take place.
Low visibility and interaction with other players outside your party.


#2 - Refined (the plan)
Outside a party:
- Every player plays their own story in their own world save. Other players are only ghosts: no NPC sync, no shared events.

In a party:
- Members borrow the party leader's story state (switches, variables, self switches) while partied, and get their own back when the party ends.
- Same story state: the main story counter (variable 1001 "Overall Events Progress") and the route progress (variables 1141-1143) match when the party forms.
- While partied, what the leader's story events change is recorded. When the party ends, members who started at the same story state keep those changes in their own save; everyone else goes back to their own state.
- What a guest keeps: items, gold, EXP and levels from partied play. Story and recruitment only under the rule above, so no companion joins twice.
- Saving while partied (including the autosave when opening the menu) never writes the leader's story into a member's own save.
- Difficulty is the leader's.

Events in a party:
- Story events run in the leader's game only; everyone present sees the same dialogue, like a live battle.
- Non-story events run separately for each member: conversations, shops, the job change menu.
- An event counts as non-story when its commands only show text and choices, open shops or known menus (job change), and never change switches, variables, self switches, maps or start battles. Event pages can be checked ahead of time, so this is known before the event starts.
- Chests and items on the ground: chest state is personal, and opening one opens it for the whole party: every member who has not looted it yet receives the items and it is marked looted in their own save. Nobody loots twice, nobody is left out.
- When the leader changes maps, the party comes along. When a story event starts, members on the map are brought to the leader first.
- If the leader leaves or drops out mid-event, members get their own state back at once; progress recorded so far is kept under the rules above.

NPCs:
- The first player to enter a map is its Map Owner. NPC movement is synchronised from the Map Owner, and only between party members on that map.
- A member who enters a map later receives the Map Owner's full picture (where each NPC stands and which page it is on) before syncing continues.

Build order:
1. Map Owner NPC sync within a party.
2. Guest mode: borrowing and restoring the leader's story state, the save guard.
3. Story events in the leader's game with shared dialogue, non-story events per member, party teleports, party-wide chests.
4. Recording the leader's story changes, so members at the same story state advance permanently.


#3 - Shared story, personal rewards
A refinement of #1: the world has one story, saved on the Relay/Server, but everything a player gains stays their own.
Every switch and variable belongs to one of three kinds:
- World: story points, defeated bosses, opened paths. Saved on the Relay, the same for everyone.
- Personal: party, items, gold, HP. Saved in each player's own world save.
- Temporary: the game's scratch values (e.g. "Event General-Purpose", "General 0-8"). Never shared.
Chests are personal: the game has ~1460 chest-like events (they give items and set their own self switch), found automatically, so every player can loot every chest once.
Story rewards (companions joining, items, gold) go to every member of the world. Players who were offline receive them the next time they join.
Key story events need the online players gathered at the spot ("force a group"). Everyone present sees the same dialogue, like a live battle.
The route choices (Ilias/Alice, Part 3's great decision) are made once per world.

Downsides:
A player playing alone moves the story forward for everyone. Could be a world setting at creation: "Story advances: anyone / only together".
Sorting ~3600 named switches and ~3600 named variables into the three kinds is a large one-time job.


#4 - Personal story, shared world
Every player plays their own story in their own world save, like single player. Only what is not tied to the story is shared: positions, chat, parties, battles.
Story events are private: others see the player with an "in event" icon.

Downsides:
The story is never played together.
NPCs and events can differ between players on the same map, since their pages depend on each player's story, so NPCs only sync where both see the same page.


#5 - Catch-up (add-on to #2 or #4)
When two players at different story points form a party, the one behind may take over the leader's story state: only the story switches and variables (the "world" kind of #3), never their party, items or gold. They are asked first.
From then on, both advance together while partied, as #2 describes for equal story states.

Downsides:
The player who catches up skips the story in between.
Progress drifts apart again whenever they play separately.


#6 - Story checkpoints
The story is split at the game's chapter boundaries. Each player keeps their own story (#4), but the world remembers the furthest chapter anyone reached.
A player may start the next chapter from the world's checkpoint whenever the group meets up, which works like a catch-up (#5) at fixed points instead of at any moment.

Downsides:
Coarser than #5: players meet on the same chapter, not the same scene.
Needs a list of the chapter boundaries (switches or variables that mark them).
